# Purpose — draft

**Fundamental aims expressed by the initiator:** emancipation of individuals and collectives, and the disappearance of relations of domination. These are guiding orientations for discussion, **not collectively ratified conclusions**.

Emancipation is understood as an ongoing individual and collective process; it is not a finished state. Anarchy is treated as a theoretical horizon whose conditions may change and whose **definitive stabilization cannot be guaranteed**, rather than a fixed institutional endpoint.

**Research purpose:** investigate economic, political, and organizational systems; compare and, where appropriate, test alternatives; document evidence, objections, unintended effects, and revisions.

**Editorial purpose:** develop a living, accessible manifesto that reflects and can be transformed by this collective inquiry.

The detailed definitions, safeguards, and unresolved questions are in the [discussion paper on aims, emancipation, and non-domination](aims-emancipation-and-non-domination.md).

## Questions still open

- How can individual and collective emancipation be assessed without imposing a model of the good life?
- What makes a power arbitrary, and what counts as effective contestation?
- How should tensions between emancipation and non-domination be analyzed?
- How can the project's own governance be genuinely contestable and revisable?
