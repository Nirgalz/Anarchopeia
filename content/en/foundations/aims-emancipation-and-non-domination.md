# Aims, emancipation and non-domination — discussion paper

**Status:** working document for public discussion, based on conversations with the project's initiator on 16 November 2026. The orientations below reflect that conversation; they have **not** been collectively ratified and may be criticized or revised. Proposed formulations and open questions are identified separately.

**Editorial note:** English is the reference language for this document's translations. [Version française](../../fr/foundations/aims-emancipation-and-non-domination.md).

## 1. Two fundamental aims

Anarchopoeia's two fundamental aims, as stated by its initiator, are:

1. **The emancipation of individuals and collectives.**
2. **The disappearance of relations of domination.**

They are related but not interchangeable. An absence of domination does not, on its own, guarantee the material, social, intellectual, or other effective conditions for emancipation. Conversely, expanding a group's collective capacity to act does not necessarily emancipate each member, and may even reinforce internal domination.

Neither aim automatically overrides the other. Conflicts between them should be examined and documented rather than hidden behind an abstract notion of the common good.

## 2. Anarchy as a horizon, not a final institutional model

The project treats **anarchy as a theoretical ideal** that can guide inquiry and transformation, not as a fixed system to be established once and for all.

Its decisive qualification is an **impossibility of definitive stabilization**, not necessarily an impossibility of realization at a given moment. Even if arrangements were to meet the conditions of an anarchic society at some point, no institution could guarantee that those conditions would remain fulfilled indefinitely.

Individuals, collectives, environments, knowledge, and technologies change. The conditions of emancipation and non-domination therefore change too. New possibilities and new mechanisms of domination can emerge. Progress is not automatic or linear; reforms may advance one aim while undermining another.

Consequently, Anarchopoeia is conceived as a **permanent, revisable process**, not a temporary stage on the way to a final constitutional, economic, or organizational order.

## 3. Emancipation: a process, individual and collective

### Working definition (open to refinement)

> **Emancipation** is an evolving, not necessarily linear individual and collective process through which people and collectives free themselves from constraints, relations of domination, dependencies, and the limiting effects of internalized beliefs and norms that restrict their freedom to think, shape themselves, determine their own ends, lead their lives, and transform their collective organization.

This is a **process**, not a completed state, a prescribed personality, or a uniform conception of a good life. It is context-dependent and multidimensional. Gains, setbacks, and tensions are possible.

**Individual emancipation** concerns people's freedom to think, form and revise convictions, shape their lives, and pursue ends they define themselves.

**Collective emancipation** concerns the ability of groups to transform the social, economic, political, cultural, or institutional conditions that constrain their members, and to determine and revise their shared organization.

These processes can reinforce each other but are not equivalent. A collective may free itself from outside control while retaining unequal or dominating relations among its members.

### Conditions, not definitions

**Autonomy** and **effective possibilities for action** are conditions for emancipation, rather than the definition of emancipation itself. Relevant material, social, intellectual, and environmental circumstances should therefore be investigated.

The **conditions for critical reflection** are also important: access to diverse knowledge, opportunities to compare arguments, and freedom to examine and revise one's own convictions. Developing personal potentialities may be an outcome of emancipation; it is not a mandatory ideal of self-improvement imposed on everyone.

### Two safeguards

1. **Not every belief, norm, dependence, or social influence is an obstacle to emancipation.** Some help people develop, cooperate, and exercise autonomy. What calls for inquiry is their limiting effect in a given context, not their mere existence.
2. **Emancipation must not be invoked to justify coercion or ideological conformity.** The project does not claim the right to decide which beliefs a person must abandon, nor which way of life is correct. It seeks to **enable critical reflection, not prescribe its conclusions**.

This also applies to the project itself: participants must be able to question its assumptions without being required to share its proposed answers.

## 4. Domination: exercised or potential, personal or systemic

### Working definition (open to refinement)

> **Domination** is a relationship or systemic configuration in which an individual or group is exposed to arbitrary power, whether exercised or credibly exercisable, arising directly or indirectly from other people, groups, organizations, institutions, or systemic mechanisms, without effective means to control, challenge, constrain, or transform that power.

The **real possibility** of arbitrary interference is relevant even when no abuse has yet occurred and no conscious intention to dominate can be identified. A merely abstract capacity to harm another person, however, is not sufficient by itself.

Domination can be:
- **Interpersonal:** one person has effectively uncontrolled arbitrary power over another.
- **Institutional:** organizational rules permit unaccountable or insufficiently contestable power.
- **Systemic:** interacting economic, political, and organizational mechanisms create dependencies or concentrations of power without a single identifiable decision-maker.

These forms may overlap. Neither every asymmetry of responsibility, nor every legitimate constraint, nor material scarcity as such necessarily constitutes domination. The institutional terms under which constraints are imposed matter.

A nominal right of appeal is insufficient if it is practically inaccessible, ineffective, dependent on the authority challenged, or dangerous to exercise. **Effective contestation** deserves explicit attention.

## 5. What Anarchopoeia aims to do

The project seeks to investigate and rethink **economic, political, and organizational systems**, as well as the interpersonal and collective relations shaped by them.

Its proposed working cycle is:

1. **Understand** existing institutions, constraints, power relations, and their effects.
2. **Distinguish** normative aims, factual observations, causal hypotheses, and proposals.
3. **Compare** explanations and alternatives, including contradictory evidence and possible harms.
4. **Develop and, where appropriate, test** institutional changes with safeguards for affected people.
5. **Evaluate** both immediate outcomes and new dependencies or possibilities of domination.
6. **Revise** proposals, methods, decision rules, and when justified, the project's own assumptions.

The manifesto should become a **public, provisional synthesis** of this process. It is not a doctrine to defend from criticism.

The project's governance should also be an object of experimentation: who can participate, who can make and revise decisions, what rights of challenge are effective, and where power remains concentrated. Its actual GitHub permissions must not be mistaken for distributed governance.

## 6. Criteria to investigate, not yet adopted metrics

To examine whether a change contributes to emancipation and non-domination, possible lines of inquiry include:

- People's real opportunities to make and revise choices.
- Accessible and effective ways to scrutinize, challenge, or change decisions.
- Effects on those who have less resources, voice, or bargaining power.
- Changes in dependencies, including unexercised powers of arbitrary interference.
- Tensions between collective gains and the emancipation of individual members.
- Unintended effects, reversibility, adaptability, and intergenerational consequences.

No single numerical score or universal model of an emancipated life is presupposed. Criteria must remain open to criticism, evidence, and contextual interpretation.

## 7. Open questions for collective research

1. What makes a power *arbitrary* in practice, and who has authority to decide?
2. Which safeguards make control and contestation genuinely effective rather than merely formal?
3. How can systemic domination be identified when no agent intends or directly controls it?
4. When individual and collective emancipation conflict, how can the conflict be examined without automatically privileging either?
5. How should internalized norms be studied without judging people's convictions on their behalf?
6. How can institutional change be assessed without prescribing one model of the good life or pretending that progress is always linear?
7. How should Anarchopoeia distribute real editorial and governance authority while remaining open to revision?

## 8. Intellectual connections to examine

These ideas have substantial precedents and should **not** be claimed as theoretically original without further research. Possible lines of comparison include human and collective emancipation (Marx), critical theory and internalized domination, autonomy and revisable institutions (Castoriadis), freedom as non-domination (Pettit), dialogical critical education (Freire), intellectual emancipation (Rancière), relational autonomy, and the capabilities approach.

These are **research leads, not an approved bibliography or claims that these approaches agree**. Sources, disagreements, and conceptual differences should be documented in the [sources area](../sources/README.md).
