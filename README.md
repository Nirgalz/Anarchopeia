# Anarchopeia

**A living, revisable manifesto developed through collective research and discussion.**

Anarchopeia is a project exploring emancipation of individuals and collectives and the disappearance of relations of domination. Its working ideas, including its foundational concepts and methods, are open to criticism and revision. Its initiator understands anarchy as a theoretical horizon whose definitive stabilization cannot be guaranteed, not as a final institution.

**Languages:** English (canonical editorial language) · [Français](README.fr.md). Translations in other languages are welcome.

## The editorial workflow

One **proposal** is the unit of research, debate and decision. Its own document contains the proposal, limitations, evaluation, proposed manifesto entry, research, references, and revision history. Every substantive change to the manifesto—including its aims, principles and definitions—must pass through a proposal.

## Proposals by category

- **[Anarchopeia](proposals/categories/anarchopeia/en.md)** — Aims, foundational principles, concepts, methods, operations and organization of the project itself. (3 primary proposals.)
- **[Glossary](proposals/categories/glossary/en.md)** — Definitions and conceptual distinctions used throughout the manifesto; their scope and implications are open to discussion and revision. (3 primary proposals.)
- **[Governance](proposals/categories/governance/en.md)** — Organization of collective decision-making, distribution of powers, institutions, participation, delegation, oversight and transformation of governance systems. (3 primary proposals.)
- **[Economy](proposals/categories/economy/en.md)** — Organization of production, labor, exchanges, money, ownership, resources and the satisfaction of material needs. Ecological issues and the material conditions of life are given primary consideration. (15 primary proposals.)
- **[Information](proposals/categories/information/en.md)** — Creation, acquisition, transmission, processing, sharing and use of information and knowledge. This includes education, sciences, media, communication, the quality of public debate, critical thinking, information systems and digital tools. (3 primary proposals.)

Each proposal appears under one primary category; category pages also list proposals associated through secondary categories. The [full catalogue](proposals/README.md) also shows statuses.

Of the 27 proposals, **AP-0001 v0.4, AP-0002 v0.4 and AP-0003 v0.5 are `approved`** and published in the manifesto; the remaining 24 are `in_progress`. All approvals are explicit editorial decisions by the project initiator, not collective ratifications.
