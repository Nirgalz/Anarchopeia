**Editorial note:** This proposal records one option drawn from an earlier article. It is open to revision, not a collectively endorsed conclusion.

## Problem

Concentrated power may enable arbitrary interference and obscure responsibility. The initiating article calls for an 'atomization' of power, but the term needs operational definition.

## Objectives

Explore ways to distribute, limit and subject decision-making authority to effective scrutiny while preserving the ability to coordinate collective action.

## Proposal

Study arrangements that divide authority among accountable actors, allow meaningful challenge and revision, and prevent enduring concentrations. Distinguish the power to act collectively from arbitrary power over people; test whether maximal fragmentation is always desirable.

## Alternatives

Separation of powers; decentralization; federations; sortition; participatory councils; delegated but recallable mandates; independent oversight.

## Criticism and limitations

Extreme dispersion may slow urgent decisions, blur responsibility or favor informal elites. Formal rights of participation may be inaccessible without resources and safeguards.

## Evaluation

Assess effective contestability, transparency, accountability, concentration of informal influence, decision quality, responsiveness and safeguards against retaliation.

## Proposed Manifesto Entry

Political, economic and organizational powers should be distributed, limited and subject to effective scrutiny. Those affected by decisions must have practical ways to question, contest and transform the authority exercised over them. Coordination and delegated responsibilities remain possible when they do not establish unaccountable arbitrary power.

## Research

Examine the distinction between delegation and domination; compare polycentric governance and accountability mechanisms; investigate the risk of informal concentration and emergency exceptions; link to AP-0003.

### Historical notes to investigate — not adopted

The [economic-political notes](../../txt/ecopolitique.txt) propose **one person, one vote**, citizen-initiated texts and issue-by-issue, revocable delegation to experts or representatives. Monthly votes and a 2% endorsement threshold are **illustrations, not adopted rules**; study access, turnout, minority protections, confidentiality and manipulation risk. The [critique of political identification](../../txt/critiques.txt) favors examining arguments over personalities. Cross-reference the new delegated-voting proposal.
