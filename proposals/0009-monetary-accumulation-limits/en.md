**Editorial note:** This proposal records one option drawn from an earlier article. It is open to revision, not a collectively endorsed conclusion.

## Problem

Concentrated monetary holdings may create asymmetries, but monetary wealth is only one component of wealth. The article proposes depreciation and destruction of currency to limit accumulation.

## Objectives

Examine whether monetary design can prevent excessive hoarding or concentrations of economic power without causing harmful avoidance and instability.

## Proposal

Investigate time-based depreciation (demurrage), expiry and destruction rules for particular monetary units. Define whether these mechanisms affect cash balances, all holdings or newly created money, and how they interact with savings and investment.

## Alternatives

Progressive taxation of wealth or income, monetary issuance rules, anti-concentration regulation, commons-based ownership and combinations.

## Criticism and limitations

Users may shift into real estate, financial assets or alternative currencies. Depreciation could penalize low-income savers or undermine trust and price stability.

## Evaluation

Model distribution by wealth level, asset substitution, transaction volume, price effects and institutional control over changing monetary rules.

## Proposed Manifesto Entry

Anarchopoeia examines monetary mechanisms such as depreciation, expiry or cancellation of currency units as possible ways to limit excessive monetary accumulation. Such mechanisms should be assessed for their fairness, economic effects, potential for avoidance and the accountability of institutions that administer them.

## Research

Compare historical demurrage schemes and monetary models; examine distributional effects, asset substitution and interactions with universal income.
