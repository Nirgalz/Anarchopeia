## Problem

Defining emancipation only as liberation from constraints or domination overlooks the **positive development of effective opportunities for self-determination**, including intellectual tools for examining one's beliefs and representations. Yet equating every increase in resources or education with emancipation risks imposing an intellectual ideal or a prescribed way of life. Individual and group emancipation, the conditions of change and its effects should be distinguished without collapsing emancipation into non-domination (AP-0003).

## Objectives

Define an evolving, recursive **individual or collective process** of self-determination; make reflective autonomy a dimension of emancipation without prescribed beliefs or intellectual thresholds; recognize that a group can **retain** shared norms after examining them; assess group, member and domination effects separately.

## Proposal

### Working definition

> **Emancipation** is an evolving, not necessarily linear individual or collective process through which people or groups develop or regain their **effective opportunities to define, pursue and revise their own ends**, direct their choices and act on their conditions of existence, including by examining and, where appropriate, changing the constraints and relationships that restrict those opportunities.

Emancipation has a **positive dimension**: it is not merely release from all constraints or domination. Material emancipation may be real without exhausting all its dimensions. Domination as defined in [AP-0003](../0003-domination/en.md) may end without all conditions for emancipation being present; new opportunities may also emerge while domination persists. The aims in [AP-0001](../0001-aims/en.md) remain distinct and equally important.

### Individual reflective autonomy

**Reflective autonomy is a dimension of emancipation, not merely an external precondition.** It concerns opportunities to acquire and use intellectual tools to examine beliefs, representations, interpretations, assumptions, social influences and ends; compare perspectives; distinguish evidence, hypotheses and arguments; and maintain or revise convictions. It is not reducible to the amount of knowledge acquired, and includes questioning interpretive frameworks.

**Rights, dignity and the legitimacy of personal choices do not depend on intellectual achievement or proficiency.** Supporting reflection grants no expert or educator authority to prescribe “correct” beliefs. A person who retains a conviction after examination does not thereby demonstrate a lack of emancipation.

### Collective reflective autonomy

**Collective emancipation** is a process through which a group develops effective opportunities to define, pursue and revise its ends, organize itself and transform its conditions of existence. It includes **collective reflection**: members develop ways to examine shared beliefs, representations and interpretations, norms, customs and habits, and the **social pressures they exert or experience**.

A group may decide to **retain, change or abandon** a practice. **Retaining a norm after critical examination can contribute to collective emancipation just as changing it can.** This does not prove that discussion was genuinely free or that domination is absent: a majority might preserve a custom while pressuring minorities. Examine opportunities for participation, dissent, challenge and revision; unanimity is not required for every decision.

Shared group ends emerge through members' relationships, interactions and decision arrangements; they do not imply a group will independent of its members. **Group emancipation does not automatically emancipate every member**. Assess individual effects and internal and external domination separately.

### Recursive conditions, processes and effects

**Analytically distinguish** enabling conditions (resources, time, security, information and competing arguments), the process (learning, scrutiny, deliberation, choices and changes of practice) and effects (new opportunities, revised representations or institutions). Roles change with time: **an effect may become the condition for further emancipation**, or create obstacles, pressures and regression. Autonomy and effective opportunities can be conditions, dimensions of a process or effects, depending on context. No universal score, fixed endpoint or necessary upward progression is assumed.

### Safeguards

**Not every belief, dependency, norm, custom or social influence is inherently alienating.** Some support individuals and groups. Their role must be examined contextually. Emancipation prescribes no identity or lifestyle, and requires neither absolute independence nor perpetual self-improvement. Reflective tools should be accessible, not turned into tests for recognizing people's rights. Purportedly emancipatory efforts remain open to challenge under [AP-0003](../0003-domination/en.md).

## Alternatives

- Define emancipation solely as freedom from external oppression.
- Define emancipation as independence from all social influence.
- Define a universally preferred emancipated identity or way of life.
- Treat collective liberation as automatically sufficient for each member's emancipation.

These approaches should be compared with the process-based definition.

**Theoretical alternatives compared:** **legal emancipation** grants a status or rights without ensuring effective opportunities to exercise them; Marx's **human emancipation** challenges social relations beyond legal rights; **Freire** integrates conscientization, dialogue and action; **Rancière** presupposes intellectual equality rather than making it conditional on instruction; **Castoriadis** emphasizes reflective self-institution; **capabilities** concern substantive opportunities; and **relational autonomy** stresses social conditions. No theory is wholesale adopted; see the Research section for agreements and differences.

## Criticism and limitations

- What counts as a "limiting" belief may be contested; outsiders may misidentify people's commitments as alienation.
- Internalized norms cannot be understood outside social relationships, and not every chosen dependence is harmful.
- An open-ended process is harder to measure than a fixed end state.
- Collective and individual emancipation may conflict.
- Encouraging critical thought must not turn into ideological training.

- **Overextension:** new knowledge, resources or rights do not automatically establish emancipation; examine their bearing on opportunities to define, pursue and revise ends.
- **Paternalism:** teaching “proper” thought can reintroduce intellectual hierarchy. Equal rights are not conditional on certified reflective competence.
- **Internalized preferences:** oppressive norms can be endorsed after reflection, but external observers can also falsely label freely held convictions as alienation. Procedural and substantive autonomy accounts disagree.
- **Apparent group reflection:** a majority may preserve a custom after discussion while silencing or marginalizing dissenters.
- **Nonuniform collective agency:** collective gains may benefit members unevenly or disadvantage outsiders.
- **Ambivalent recursion:** new opportunities may create further domination or reduce others' opportunities; neither novelty nor conservation is automatically progress.
- **Unequal access:** time, resources, language, educational opportunity, information, safety and protection against retaliation affect the use of reflective tools.

## Evaluation

Possible questions to study, not pre-approved measurements:

- Can individuals actually question and revise decisions, norms and convictions without retaliation?
- Do institutions expand effective opportunities without prescribing what people should do with them?
- Who defines a collective's progress, and can dissenting members challenge that definition?
- Are new forms of dependence or pressure emerging during ostensibly emancipatory change?
- Can shifts be identified across material, social and cognitive dimensions without assuming that progress is linear?

### Boundary criterion: gaining means versus emancipation

**An additional resource, skill, item of knowledge or option is not by itself sufficient to establish emancipation.** To characterize a change, look for evidence connecting it to a **genuine and relevant** change in opportunities to **define, pursue or revise one's own ends**. These three aspects are not a required performance checklist: a transformation may affect one without necessarily affecting all the others.

Examine separately: **(a)** whether the new option is practically accessible rather than merely formal, **(b)** its relevance to the self-determination of the people or group concerned, **(c)** any pressures, prescriptions and dependencies restricting autonomous use, and **(d)** effects on other people and group members. An unused option can still be meaningful; a quantitative increase in options alone proves nothing; pursuing ends prescribed by an external authority does not, by itself, establish emancipation.

**These are prompts for inquiry, not a universal test or a condition for recognizing anyone's rights.** The classification may remain indeterminate when facts are insufficient or accounts conflict. Assess any relation of domination separately under AP-0003.

**Additional boundary tests:** formal rights without material means; improved resources accompanied by an inability to question a belief; the same custom retained after open, contestable deliberation or under heavy social pressure; a group becomes externally autonomous while excluding members; technical training leaves the relevant choices unchanged. In each case, assess material change, reflection, group agency, member opportunities and domination separately, and identify effects that may become new enabling conditions. These are **not validated empirical measures**; some conclusions may remain undetermined.

## Proposed Manifesto Entry

**Emancipation** is an evolving, not necessarily linear individual or collective process through which people and groups develop or regain effective opportunities to define, pursue and revise their own ends, direct their choices and act on their conditions of existence. It is not limited to overcoming constraints or ending relations of domination.

It includes a **reflective dimension**: opportunities to acquire and use tools for examining one's beliefs, representations, interpretations and their influence on choices. For a group, this also includes examining its shared norms, customs, habits and the social pressures it exerts or experiences. This may lead to retaining, changing or abandoning practices after reflection.

Emancipation prescribes no belief, lifestyle or level of intellectual development to attain. Material, social and institutional conditions interact with opportunities for reflection: **the effects of one emancipatory process may enable further emancipation**. The emancipation of a group does not guarantee that of each member; their opportunities and relations of domination must be examined separately.

## Research


### Comparative research on definitions of emancipation — 16 November 2026

The [Académie française](https://www.dictionnaire-academie.fr/article/A9E0843) distinguishes **legal**, **social** and **intellectual** senses of emancipation. **Simon Susen** describes transitions from heteronomy to autonomy and from dependency to freedom without proposing a universal definition ([publisher's abstract](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/9781118474396.wbept0307)). AP-0002 retains liberation but **extends emancipation to positive, reflective and recursive self-determination**.

| Approach | Convergence with AP-0002 | Difference / objection |
|---|---|---|
| **Marx** — political vs human emancipation [3] | Formal rights do not exhaust social change. | His historical and socioeconomic critique is more specific than AP-0002's general definition. |
| **Critical theory** [4] | Critique of social structures and internalized forms of power. | Some approaches emphasize systemic overhaul; AP-0002 also allows partial, local and revisable change. |
| **Freire** — conscientization and praxis [5] | Dialogue, critical reflection and an iterative reflection/action cycle. | Praxis emphasizes changing oppression; AP-0002 also recognizes a reflective decision to **retain** a norm, without thereby declaring it just. |
| **Rancière** — intellectual emancipation [6] | Resistance to expert domination and hierarchies of knowledge. | **Intellectual equality is presupposed from the outset**, not achieved by passing an educational test. Reflective tools cannot become prerequisites for equal rights or recognition. |
| **Castoriadis** — autonomy and self-institution [7] | Close resemblance: a society questions its own rules, customs, institutions and ends. | His account of democratic self-institution is more specific; AP-0002 separates group and member emancipation. |
| **Sen / Nussbaum** — capabilities [8] | Distinguish rights, resources, effective opportunities and functionings; consider conversion factors. | The approach primarily examines opportunities, while AP-0002 defines a **reflective process**; no capability metric is adopted. |
| **Relational autonomy** [9] | Socialization, recognition and social pressures shape self-determination. | Disagreement between **procedural** and **substantive** autonomy when restrictive norms are deliberately endorsed. |
| **Feminist approaches to power** [10] | Distinguish power over others from opportunities for action; account for internalized oppression. | AP-0002 remains distinct from the specific definition of domination in AP-0003. |
| **Social norms** [11] | Group habits, expectations and sanctions may be reproduced without examination. | Voting or declaring awareness does not guarantee freedom to dissent. |
| **Collective intentionality** [12] | Common decisions and goals emerge through coordinated arrangements and commitments. | Do not posit a group will independent of the members and their relations. |

**Analytical conclusions:** the proposed definition is not without precedent: Castoriadis, Freire and relational-autonomy work are especially close. It explicitly combines **positive self-determination**, **individual and collective reflection**, the **possibility of retaining norms after examination**, **recursion** and **separate assessment of the group and its members**. This combination does not by itself establish scientific novelty.

**Conceptual orientations explicitly accepted by the initiator:** positive development of opportunities; reflective autonomy as constitutive without intellectual threshold or prescribed conclusions; effects becoming conditions for further emancipation; group emancipation distinguished from member emancipation; the group's reflection on shared beliefs, social pressures, norms, customs and habits, including the possibility of **retaining** them. **Acceptance of these choices does not constitute formal editorial approval of AP-0002.**

**Limits:** qualitative, non-systematic literature review; several complete works not directly consulted; distinguish secondary from primary material. The comparisons are AP-0002's analysis, not exhaustive claims attributed to the authors or empirical validation.

Research directions to develop within this proposal:

- Compare human emancipation, critical theory, autonomy, relational autonomy and intellectual emancipation.
- Examine the difference between empowering critical reflection and prescribing the conclusions of reflection.
- Investigate how socialization, internalized norms, dependence and institutional structures interact.
- Consider the capabilities approach as a way to analyze effective opportunities, without equating it to the definition of emancipation.
- Study cases where collective autonomy increased while members' opportunities or rights decreased.

### Historical notes to investigate — not adopted

The [older text on potential and action](https://github.com/Nirgalz/Anarchopeia/blob/0d8963af515be54957f767531017aff3d6881955/txt/puissance%20pouvoir%20et%20resolution.txt) distinguishes aspirations, enabling conditions and the constraints of collective action. Its terminology precedes the more recent **Méthode trialectique**, a separate project, and must not be imported as an established theory. The [practical philosophy notes](https://github.com/Nirgalz/Anarchopeia/blob/0d8963af515be54957f767531017aff3d6881955/txt/philosophie%20pratique.txt) explore introspection and contextual understanding as optional aids to self-reflection, **not compulsory psychological training**. Research how people discover and revise personal aims without a productivity imperative.
