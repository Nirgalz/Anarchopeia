## Problem

If emancipation is reduced to an achieved state, an imposed ideal of the emancipated person, or mere absence of an external authority, the project risks overlooking internalized constraints, material conditions, and conflicts between individuals and groups.

## Objectives

Define emancipation as a **process**, distinguish its individual and collective dimensions, identify conditions that enable it, and guard against coercion in its name.

## Proposal

### Working definition

> **Emancipation** is an evolving, not necessarily linear **individual and collective process** through which individuals and groups free themselves from constraints, relations of domination, dependencies, and limiting effects of internalized beliefs and norms that restrict their freedom to think, shape themselves, determine their own ends, lead their lives, or transform their collective organization.

Emancipation is **not a final state**. It is contextual and multidimensional; advances, setbacks and tensions are possible as people and environments evolve.

**Individual emancipation** concerns individuals' processes of becoming freer to think, question their convictions, shape their lives and pursue ends they define for themselves.

**Collective emancipation** concerns groups transforming the social, economic, political, cultural and institutional conditions that restrict their members, and becoming able to determine and revise their own organization.

These processes interact but are not identical. A group may free itself from outside control while maintaining domination within it; collective emancipation does not automatically entail the emancipation of every member.

### Conditions, not the definition

**Autonomy** and **effective opportunities for action** are conditions for the realization of emancipation, **not definitions of emancipation**. Material, intellectual, social and environmental conditions matter in context.

The project seeks to foster the **conditions for critical reflection**: access to diverse knowledge, opportunities to confront arguments, and freedom to examine and revise convictions. It does **not** determine which beliefs someone must discard or which conclusions are correct.

Developing and expressing potentialities may result from emancipation; it must not become an externally imposed demand for self-improvement.

### Two safeguards to preserve

1. **Not all beliefs, norms, dependencies or social influences are inherently alienating.** Some provide bearings, support autonomy and enable cooperation. Their potentially limiting effects require contextual inquiry.
2. **Emancipation cannot justify imposing a belief, value, or way of life.** Anarchopoeia must avoid reproducing domination while purporting to liberate people.

These safeguards apply to how members of the project interact with one another.

Related proposals: [Project aims](../0001-aims/en.md) and [Domination](../0003-domination/en.md).

## Alternatives

- Define emancipation solely as freedom from external oppression.
- Define emancipation as independence from all social influence.
- Define a universally preferred emancipated identity or way of life.
- Treat collective liberation as automatically sufficient for each member's emancipation.

These approaches should be compared with the process-based definition.

## Criticism and limitations

- What counts as a "limiting" belief may be contested; outsiders may misidentify people's commitments as alienation.
- Internalized norms cannot be understood outside social relationships, and not every chosen dependence is harmful.
- An open-ended process is harder to measure than a fixed end state.
- Collective and individual emancipation may conflict.
- Encouraging critical thought must not turn into ideological training.

## Evaluation

Possible questions to study, not pre-approved measurements:

- Can individuals actually question and revise decisions, norms and convictions without retaliation?
- Do institutions expand effective opportunities without prescribing what people should do with them?
- Who defines a collective's progress, and can dissenting members challenge that definition?
- Are new forms of dependence or pressure emerging during ostensibly emancipatory change?
- Can shifts be identified across material, social and cognitive dimensions without assuming that progress is linear?

## Proposed Manifesto Entry

**Emancipation** is an evolving, not necessarily linear **individual and collective process** through which people and collectives free themselves from constraints, relations of domination, dependencies, and the limiting effects of internalized beliefs and norms that restrict their freedom to think, shape their lives, define their own ends, or transform their collective organization.

Autonomy and effective opportunities to act are **conditions of emancipation**, not its definition. Not every belief, norm, dependence or social influence is an obstacle to emancipation. Anarchopoeia seeks to foster the conditions for critical reflection without prescribing which beliefs people must abandon, which conclusions they must reach, or how they must live.

Individual and collective emancipation may reinforce one another, but the emancipation of a collective does not automatically guarantee the emancipation of each of its members.

## Research

Research directions to develop within this proposal:

- Compare human emancipation, critical theory, autonomy, relational autonomy and intellectual emancipation.
- Examine the difference between empowering critical reflection and prescribing the conclusions of reflection.
- Investigate how socialization, internalized norms, dependence and institutional structures interact.
- Consider the capabilities approach as a way to analyze effective opportunities, without equating it to the definition of emancipation.
- Study cases where collective autonomy increased while members' opportunities or rights decreased.

### Historical notes to investigate — not adopted

The [older text on potential and action](https://github.com/Nirgalz/Anarchopeia/blob/0d8963af515be54957f767531017aff3d6881955/txt/puissance%20pouvoir%20et%20resolution.txt) distinguishes aspirations, enabling conditions and the constraints of collective action. Its terminology precedes the more recent **Méthode trialectique**, a separate project, and must not be imported as an established theory. The [practical philosophy notes](https://github.com/Nirgalz/Anarchopeia/blob/0d8963af515be54957f767531017aff3d6881955/txt/philosophie%20pratique.txt) explore introspection and contextual understanding as optional aids to self-reflection, **not compulsory psychological training**. Research how people discover and revise personal aims without a productivity imperative.
