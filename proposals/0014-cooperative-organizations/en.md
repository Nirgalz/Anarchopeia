**Editorial note:** This proposal records one option drawn from an earlier article. It is open to revision, not a collectively endorsed conclusion.

## Problem

Conventional employment and corporate governance may concentrate control over work while limiting workers' voice. The initiating text advocates making cooperatives widespread.

## Objectives

Assess cooperative models as a means to increase participation, fairer governance and accountability in work and production.

## Proposal

Study how workers and affected stakeholders could establish, convert or federate cooperative organizations. Identify legal structures, rights of nonmembers, access to capital and arrangements for managing internal disputes.

## Alternatives

Conventional firms with codetermination, public enterprises, employee ownership, worker cooperatives, multistakeholder cooperatives and hybrids.

## Criticism and limitations

Cooperatives can reproduce exclusions or informal leadership hierarchies and face difficulties raising funds or surviving downturns. No model guarantees emancipation.

## Evaluation

Compare participation, labor security, governance transparency, pay distribution, longevity, investment needs and responsiveness to customers and communities.

## Proposed Manifesto Entry

Anarchopoeia explores the expansion of cooperative work and production, enabling those who contribute to and are affected by an activity to exercise meaningful influence over its organization. Cooperative governance must itself remain accountable, inclusive and open to challenge.

## Research

Compare worker and multistakeholder cooperatives; study conversion barriers, investment finance and democratic practice; examine economic performance and internal inequalities.
