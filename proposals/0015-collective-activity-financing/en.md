**Editorial note:** This proposal records one option drawn from an earlier article. It is open to revision, not a collectively endorsed conclusion.

## Problem

Activities valued by a community may struggle to obtain investment when funders require private profit. The initial text seeks alternatives to dependence on banks, states or individual investors.

## Objectives

Investigate ways for people affected by an activity to participate in funding and assessing it, without making profitability the only condition of existence.

## Proposal

Compare community funding, participatory investment, mutual funds, cooperative finance and non-profit grants. Specify who may contribute, who allocates funds, how projects are evaluated and how conflicts of interest are handled.

## Alternatives

Bank lending, venture investment, public subsidy, participatory budgeting, community bonds, cooperative finance and blended funding.

## Criticism and limitations

Popularity is not proof of usefulness. Funding based on donations can favor affluent groups or fashionable projects, while shared budgets face scarcity and monitoring difficulties.

## Evaluation

Evaluate access for underrepresented applicants, funding stability, transparency, independence of decision-makers and public benefit against stated criteria.

## Proposed Manifesto Entry

Anarchopoeia explores collective financing mechanisms through which people can support activities they consider useful, including activities that do not primarily seek financial profit. The allocation of funds must be transparent, accountable and designed to prevent economic or social influence from becoming arbitrary power.

## Research

Examine crowdfunding, mutual finance, participatory budgets and cooperative funds; compare allocation criteria, legal constraints and safeguards against donor capture.

### Historical notes to investigate — not adopted

The [economic-political proposal notes](../../txt/ecopolitique.txt) detail **equal, periodically allocated investment credits** used to prioritize citizen-proposed projects, without a monetary return; projects may receive one-off allocations or recurring subscriptions. Distinguish these credits from consumption money and document whether transfer of allocation rights to a third party is allowed, how such delegation is audited and how minorities get funding. Consider large infrastructure funded by future commitments, but examine revocability, non-performance and obligations. Cross-reference the independent proposal for collective investment rights.
