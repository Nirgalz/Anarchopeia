**Editorial note:** This proposal records one option drawn from an earlier article. It is open to revision, not a collectively endorsed conclusion.

## Problem

Market prices may fail to reflect some harms borne by people or ecosystems other than those buying and selling. The source article proposes internalizing such costs.

## Objectives

Explore methods to make negative effects of production and resource use visible, preventable and fairly accounted for.

## Proposal

Assess pricing mechanisms as well as regulation, caps, collective liability, public disclosure and commons governance. Not all harms can or should be expressed as a single monetary price.

## Alternatives

Pollution taxes, permits, direct standards, bans, liability rules, public provision, ecological budgets and mixed arrangements.

## Criticism and limitations

Valuing harm monetarily can conceal rights violations or irreversible losses. Poor households may bear costs if compensation and distribution are neglected.

## Evaluation

Assess avoided harm, ecological limits, distributional effects, enforceability, measurement uncertainty and whether affected communities can challenge the rules.

## Proposed Manifesto Entry

Anarchopoeia examines how economic decisions can account for social and environmental effects imposed on others and on future generations. Depending on the harm, appropriate measures may include changes in prices, legal obligations, limits on activity or collective control, with attention to distributive justice and ecological thresholds.

## Research

Compare market-based and nonmarket approaches to externalities; study life-cycle accounting, environmental justice and uncertain or irreversible damages.

### Historical notes to investigate — not adopted

The [economic-political draft](https://github.com/Nirgalz/Anarchopeia/blob/0d8963af515be54957f767531017aff3d6881955/txt/ecopolitique.txt) proposes **localized resource prices** including extraction, transformation, transportation and costs of repairing environmental damage (for example reforestation following clearing). These are useful modeling cases, not proof that all damage can be fully compensated. Analyze **irreversibility, threshold effects, collective consent and the limits of monetary substitution**. Supply-chain transparency may also reveal harms that are invisible in final prices.
