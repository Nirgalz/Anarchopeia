**Editorial note:** This proposal records one option drawn from an earlier article. It is open to revision, not a collectively endorsed conclusion.

## Problem

The organization of productive assets affects who can set priorities, receive the benefits of production, and control working conditions. The source article advocates collectivization but leaves governance unspecified.

## Objectives

Compare forms of shared ownership and control that could support emancipation while preventing new arbitrary hierarchies.

## Proposal

Examine worker, community, municipal and commons-oriented ownership arrangements for means of production. Distinguish ownership from management, investment decisions, access and accountability. Do not assume a single model fits all sectors.

## Alternatives

Regulated private ownership, public enterprises, worker cooperatives, mixed ownership, trusts and commons stewardship.

## Criticism and limitations

Collectivization alone does not ensure democratic control; financial risk, investment incentives, exclusion of nonmembers and incumbent managers' power must be examined.

## Evaluation

Assess effective participation, allocation of benefits and risks, working conditions, productive performance, access for newcomers and ability to contest or change governance.

## Proposed Manifesto Entry

Anarchopoeia examines collective and cooperative forms of ownership and governance of productive assets. Their value depends not only on who legally owns the resources but also on who can decide, benefit, contest decisions and revise the rules governing production.

## Research

Study ownership versus use and governance; compare experience in coops and community enterprises; analyze investment, transition and minority protections.
