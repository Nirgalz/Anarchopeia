**Editorial note:** This proposal records one option drawn from an earlier article. It is open to revision, not a collectively endorsed conclusion.

## Problem

Education can be organized around labor-market adaptation rather than inquiry, self-determination and critical reflection. The source article proposes a broader emancipatory orientation.

## Objectives

Examine educational environments that develop critical inquiry, access to knowledge and opportunities to discover interests without prescribing a vocation or a correct worldview.

## Proposal

Study educational arrangements emphasizing inquiry, practical exploration, cooperation, intellectual autonomy and diverse life paths. Treat preparation for employment as one possible educational function, not the exhaustive purpose of education.

## Alternatives

Vocational education, general liberal education, democratic schooling, project-based learning, public curricula and hybrid approaches.

## Criticism and limitations

Claims of emancipation can mask indoctrination or social privilege. Critical reflection requires access, teacher support and safeguards against imposing ideological conclusions.

## Evaluation

Study reasoning skills, inclusion, learner voice, access to varied knowledge, well-being and freedom to revise educational and vocational choices.

## Proposed Manifesto Entry

Education should foster the conditions for critical reflection, discovery and self-determination, rather than reduce learners to their future roles in the labor market. An emancipatory approach provides access to diverse knowledge and meaningful opportunities to explore interests, question norms and revise choices, without imposing a prescribed vocation or set of beliefs.

## Research

Examine critical pedagogy, democratic education, intellectual emancipation and vocational pluralism; investigate how access and inequalities influence educational autonomy; connect to AP-0002.
