**Editorial status:** **AP-0003 v0.5 was explicitly approved by the initiator on 16 November 2026** and its candidate text is included in the [manifesto](../../manifesto/en.md). This is an editorial decision, not collective ratification or empirical validation.

## Problem

Defining domination only through observed abuse overlooks credible but unexercised arbitrary powers, structural dependencies and configurations in which no single actor intends to control the whole outcome. Conversely, treating every constraint, dependency or asymmetry as domination would erase the difference between justifiable rules and arbitrary mechanisms. The proposal therefore needs both a **normative definition** and **context-specific investigative criteria**, without conflating the two.

## Objectives

Define interpersonal, institutional and systemic domination; clarify arbitrariness, credible potential interference and effective safeguards; enable investigation of the mechanisms involved and comparison of possible changes, without presuming that any solution eliminates all domination or making the manifesto dependent on an external methodology.

## Proposal

### Working definition

> **Domination** is a relationship or systemic configuration in which an individual or group is genuinely and credibly exposed to **arbitrary power**, whether exercised or credibly exercisable, over their opportunities to act or conditions of existence.

**Arbitrariness** is assessed through three complementary requirements: **(1) justification** of rules and decisions to affected people; **(2) effective limits** on how power may be exercised; and **(3) effective control**, enabling accountability, challenge and reconsideration or change of decisions or rules. **No single requirement necessarily rules out arbitrariness on its own**; their adequacy must be evaluated together in context. Reasons invoked by an authority, legality, majority approval, consent and purely formal remedies do not, by themselves, guarantee the absence of arbitrariness. These are contestable normative criteria, not conclusions mechanically inferred from an action's material conditions.

Domination may be **interpersonal** (relations between people), **institutional** (organizational rules and practices) or **systemic** (interacting economic, political, social or informational mechanisms). These forms may overlap. Systemic domination requires neither a single central decision-maker nor a conscious intention to dominate; identifying it nevertheless requires a **description of the relational mechanisms** that expose people to arbitrary power.

**Potential domination** means exposure to **credible, context-specific arbitrary interference**, even when no interference has occurred. A merely conceivable or abstract ability to cause harm is insufficient. Relevant conditions include actually available opportunities, conditions for their exercise, practical barriers and safeguards available to affected people.

**Dependency is not a necessary condition of every relation of domination.** It may nevertheless heighten exposure to arbitrary power, aggravate its effects and restrict realistic options for escaping it. Dependency and exit costs therefore matter contextually rather than as universal prerequisites. **Not every power asymmetry, dependency, difference in expertise, collective constraint or material scarcity is necessarily domination.** Specialized authority may be constrained and accountable; a collective rule may impose a necessary constraint without being arbitrary. Rules allocating scarce resources can nevertheless establish domination.

**Whether a relationship is domination and how intense it is are distinct questions.** Once its classification has been examined, the magnitude, frequency, duration, credibility of interference and severity of effects may vary by context. No universal domination score is assumed.

### Investigating mechanisms and possible changes

The analysis should specify, for a defined context and period, the affected people and groups; relevant actors, organizations and arrangements; dependency relations; conditions that make interference credible; and effective means of control, challenge, limitation and reform. Safeguards must be **practicable**, sufficiently independent, able to provide meaningful remedies and usable without retaliation. **Participation in shaping and changing the rules is one dimension of effective control**, exercised directly or indirectly, individually or collectively. This does not require everyone to participate personally in every decision. Distinguish participation in decisions, oversight of their exercise and opportunities to change the rules authorizing them. Delegation or specialized authority is not automatically arbitrary when it remains justifiable, limited and accountable.

Where helpful for complex cases, an analytical framework such as the [Trialectic Method](https://github.com/Nirgalz/trialectic/blob/3c3b5c831ce3afd86d7a5c654b167fe97502d30e/reference/trialectic-method.md) may inform **research**, not the normative definition. It distinguishes Objects (individuals, groups, institutions or systems), selected analytical Subjects, their Environment, **NEEDs** (necessities), **CANs** (Potentialities), conditions, Actualizations and effects. Its technical **Power** is a **contextual evaluation operation**, distinct from the political meaning of “arbitrary power” in this proposal. An available or actionable CAN is not by itself evidence of domination.

Assessment should explore several relevant scenarios: preventing arbitrary interference, improving genuinely effective remedies, reducing material dependency or changing institutions. Compare feasibility, complexity, costs and resources, temporality, expected effectiveness, effects on multiple Subjects and uncertainty without prescribing a uniquely best option. A transformation may reduce domination in one relationship while shifting power to other actors, so **recursive reassessment** is necessary. Keep predicted, simulated, observed and causally attributed effects separate.

**Conceptual independence:** the political criteria in this proposal must remain understandable, contestable and assessable **without knowing or adopting the Trialectic Method**, which remains in development and is not scientifically validated.

Related proposals: [Aims](../0001-aims/en.md) (approved v0.4), [Emancipation](../0002-emancipation/en.md) and [Distribution and oversight of power](../0006-distributed-power/en.md).

## Alternatives

- **Abuse-only definition:** domination exists only after observed interference; this may overlook vulnerability to unexercised arbitrary power.
- **Every-asymmetry definition:** any dependency or inequality of power counts as domination; this conflates constraints, expertise, coordination and arbitrariness.
- **Intentional definition:** a conscious intention to dominate is required; this overlooks institutional and systemic effects.
- **Exclusively legal definition:** legality or formal appeal procedures rule out arbitrariness; their actual effectiveness remains to be established.
- **Exclusively structural definition:** entire systems are called dominant without adequately specifying relationships and mechanisms; this is difficult to test or contest.

These alternatives warrant comparison through concrete cases and counterexamples rather than being assumed refuted.

### Comparative theoretical alternatives — research, not adopted

Academic accounts offer further alternatives, distinct from the conceptual options above:

- **Neo-republicanism (Philip Pettit):** domination primarily involves discretionary or insufficiently controlled interference power, whether exercised or not. AP-0003 is closely aligned but adds justificatory language whose normative role remains to be clarified. [Academic overview](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/republicanism/).
- **Frank Lovett:** domination combines **dependency within a social relationship** and arbitrary power; **exit costs** help characterize dependency. AP-0003 treats dependency as important but not expressly necessary. [Book overview](https://academic.oup.com/book/6656).
- **Iris Marion Young:** institutional domination restricts meaningful participation in determining people's actions or the conditions of their action. AP-0003 focuses on limits and effective remedies without specifying co-determination as necessary or sufficient. [Book](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctvcm4g4q).
- **Max Weber's `Herrschaft`:** sociological authority and obedience considered legitimate by participants do not necessarily entail moral injustice. AP-0003 reserves “domination” for politically objectionable arbitrary power. [SEP overview](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/weber/).
- **Rainer Forst and the right to justification:** equal standing in requesting and shaping the justifications of rules is central; AP-0003 invokes justifications without defining such equal standing. [Comparative overview](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/win2025/entries/domination/).

These are **objects of comparison**, not newly adopted foundations. See Research for the detailed convergences, differences and evidential limitations.

## Criticism and limitations

- **Contested normative criteria:** who decides when justification, safeguards or remedies are “sufficient”? **Justification, limits and effective control are complementary requirements**, not a mechanical checklist whose completion proves non-arbitrariness. Majorities, specialized authorities and legal procedures may themselves dominate minorities. No universal legitimacy test is settled.
- **Consent and dependency:** voluntary acceptance does not necessarily rule out domination when shaped by dependency; nor is every freely chosen dependency arbitrary. **Editorial decision:** dependency is not universally necessary; cases of episodic interference and low exit costs should still test the boundaries of the concept.
- **Credibility of interference:** distinguishing genuinely exercisable power from abstract possibilities needs contextual evidence. Lack of observed abuse proves neither the presence nor absence of domination.
- **Classification and intensity:** the severity of domination can vary, but a threshold for classification remains difficult without contextual criteria. An arbitrary numerical index would imply false precision.
- **Systemic domination:** the lack of a central actor does not warrant ascribing every harmful outcome to a “dominant” system; relations, conditions and mechanisms require evidence.
- **Insufficient formal remedies:** delays, costs, retaliation risks and lack of accessible information can make review illusory. Even effective remedies after the fact may not repair all harms. **Participation and oversight:** individual appeals may coexist with no meaningful influence over the rules, while voting participation cannot by itself protect minorities. Direct participation in every decision is not required.
- **Effects of safeguards:** protective arrangements may establish new administrative powers, dependencies or constraints affecting other people.
- **Methodological limitations:** NEEDs, CANs and Power evaluations help structure hypotheses but do not establish whether a relationship is arbitrary or whether reform works; the Method's algorithms and empirical validation remain in development.

### Additional objections raised by the literature

- **Is dependency necessary or contingent?** Lovett emphasizes the costs of meaningful exit; without a minimal dependency requirement, AP-0003 may label serious but episodic threats or interference “domination” where other theories use different categories. Requiring dependency, however, could exclude some systemic mechanisms.
- **Procedural versus substantive arbitrariness:** republican approaches distinguish reliable procedural checks from democratic control. AP-0003 combines control, limits and justification but does not settle whether persuasive reasons compensate for lack of control by affected people or whether effective procedures can legitimate substantively unjust rules.
- **Participation versus individual appeal:** Young and right-to-justification theories emphasize meaningful participation in defining the rules themselves. An effective individual appeal can coexist with systematic exclusion from rule-making.
- **Normative meaning of domination:** Weber's `Herrschaft` is primarily sociological and may be described as legitimate, unlike AP-0003's morally objectionable category. The existence of Weberian authority does not itself prove or disprove AP-0003 domination.
- **Diffuse power and normalization:** Foucauldian and feminist approaches also investigate internalized norms, subject formation and locked-in relationships. Focusing only on interference and remedies may overlook these processes; nevertheless, not every social influence is domination.
- **Overinclusive scope:** the mere opportunity to affect a trivial choice should not automatically be equated with severe vulnerability. Consider scope, credibility, exit costs, social status and lasting effects before categorizing ordinary interactions as domination.

These are **research objections to the unchanged candidate**, not resolved conclusions or newly adopted conditions.

## Evaluation

Examine **the normative classification** of the relationship separately from **the observable mechanisms**. For each significant case:

1. **Contextualize:** identify exposed people or groups, other relevant actors and institutions, relationships, Environment, applicable rules and period. What is known, hypothetical or missing from the inventory?
2. **Identify the power:** who could intervene in whose options or circumstances, and under what conditions? How credible is that intervention, separately from whether it has occurred?
3. **Assess arbitrariness:** examine **separately and jointly** (a) justification of rules and decisions to affected people, (b) effective limits on power, and (c) genuine opportunities for oversight. Can those affected demand accountability, contest decisions, obtain reconsideration or change the authorizing rules? Are safeguards accessible, effective, sufficiently independent and usable without retaliation? **No single requirement necessarily suffices**; legality or majority support alone does not settle this question.
4. **Separate classification and intensity:** what evidence supports or counters calling this domination? How great is the scope, frequency, duration or severity of exposure? How do dependency, exit costs or access to alternatives change exposure **without becoming universal prerequisites** or imposing a universal score?
5. **Investigate systemic mechanisms:** what combination of rules, resources, dependencies, practices and relations produces this exposure? What evidence might contradict the proposed explanation?
6. **Compare scenarios:** examine materially distinct options—justified preservation, prevention, improved remedies, reduced dependencies, institutional change—in terms of feasibility, complexity, costs/resources, temporality, expected effectiveness, effects on different Subjects and uncertainties. No selection is mandatory.
7. **Reassess:** distinguish direct/indirect and immediate/delayed effects, as well as anticipated, simulated, observed and causally attributed changes. Ask whether reducing domination for some introduces it elsewhere. Revise conclusions as new evidence arises.

**Claim traceability:** for consequential conclusions, record assumptions, supporting and contrary evidence, limitations, competing explanations and what remains indeterminate. A derivation valid within a model is not automatically empirical proof. No aggregate score or automatic scenario ranking is assumed.

### Literature-based counterexample tests

In addition to the evaluation operations above, test the candidate against contrasting cases:

- **Unexercised power:** credible arbitrary interference is available but not exercised. Can AP-0003 classify it without pretending it was exercised?
- **Low exit costs:** someone can leave a relation with credible arbitrary interference easily. Does the lack of dependency change the classification, degree, or expected harm? Compare Lovett.
- **Review without participation:** independent individual appeals are available but affected people have no say over the underlying rules. Compare Young and Pettit's approaches.
- **Legally recognized authority:** rules viewed as legitimate by some disadvantage others. Separate Weberian sociological legitimacy, normative justification and practical safeguards.
- **Internalized social power:** social norms structurally reduce a group's options without a single actor's explicit decision. Establish the relations, mechanisms and evidence rather than presuming arbitrariness.
- **Episodic interference:** a severe one-off threat or harm does not establish a lasting relationship. Examine whether every unjust interference must count as domination or whether a different normative category is needed.

Preserve **competing interpretations**, indicate which observations could discriminate between them, and report `indeterminate` when evidence or normative criteria cannot decide. Agreement with a school of thought is not empirical validation or editorial approval.

## Proposed Manifesto Entry

**Domination** is a relationship or systemic configuration in which an individual or group is genuinely and credibly exposed to arbitrary power, whether exercised or credibly exercisable, over their opportunities to act or conditions of existence.

Whether power is arbitrary is assessed using **three complementary requirements**: justification of rules and decisions to affected people, effective limits on the exercise of power, and genuine opportunities for oversight and challenge. No single requirement necessarily rules out arbitrariness on its own; their adequacy depends on context. Legality, majority approval or the existence of formal remedies do not, by themselves, guarantee non-arbitrariness.

Domination can be interpersonal, institutional or systemic. It requires neither an actual abuse nor a conscious intention to dominate. It may arise through interacting mechanisms without any single actor controlling the whole arrangement.

**Dependency is not a necessary condition of every relation of domination**, but it may increase exposure to arbitrary power, intensify its effects and restrict opportunities to escape it. Not every power asymmetry, difference in expertise or collective constraint necessarily constitutes domination. Analysis must examine the relationships, the conditions for exercising power and affected people's effective options. **Participation in shaping or changing rules is one dimension of effective control**: it can take direct or indirect forms and does not require personal participation in every decision.

The disappearance of relations of domination requires identifying and transforming mechanisms that allow arbitrary power to be exercised. Proposed changes must be evaluated for their effects on affected people and groups, including the creation of new dependencies or concentrations of power.

## Research

### Conceptual orientations explicitly validated — 16 November 2026

The initiator **explicitly validated** the following three orientations while reviewing differences between AP-0003 and the approaches of Lovett, Pettit, Young and Forst. These concern **AP-0003's political definition**, not empirical validation of those theories:

1. **Dependency:** not necessary in every case of domination, but it can increase exposure, aggravate effects and restrict realistic exit options.
2. **Arbitrariness:** justification, limits and effective oversight are complementary requirements; none necessarily suffices on its own. Adequacy remains context-sensitive and contestable.
3. **Participation:** one dimension of effective control, through direct or indirect influence over decisions and rules; it does not require personal involvement in every decision. Distinguish participation, oversight and changes to rules.

The following comparative literature remains **an object of research**, not a wholesale adoption of any of the theories discussed.

### Comparative research on definitions of domination — 16 November 2026

**Question:** how does the candidate definition in AP-0003 v0.4 compare with recognized accounts in political philosophy, sociology and critical theory? This review is **descriptive and critical**: it does not adopt any external theory or modify the candidate definition or editorial status.

**Overview:** AP-0003 is most closely aligned with **neo-republican non-domination** (potential, unexercised arbitrary power), while admitting **systemic configurations** and explicitly requiring **justification, limits and effective remedies**. This is a coherent normative proposal but not an exact reproduction of any one academic theory. Richard C. McCammon distinguishes moralized and non-moralized as well as norm-dependent and norm-independent theories, and competing views of agents, structures and exercised power. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, revised 2025](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/win2025/entries/domination/).

| Approach | Main definition or criterion | Agreement with AP-0003 | Divergence or open question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Philip Pettit / neo-republicanism** | Exposure to discretionary, insufficiently controlled interference power, including unexercised power | Very strong: potential power and effective checks | AP-0003 also stresses justification; how do procedural checks, democratic control and substantive reasons interact? |
| **Frank Lovett** | Dependency in social relations plus arbitrary power; exit costs matter | Strong: dependency, credibility and unexercised power | Dependency is **not explicitly necessary** in AP-0003, potentially widening its scope |
| **Iris Marion Young** | Structures preventing reciprocal participation in determining conduct and its conditions | Strong on structural domination and lack of control | AP-0003 does **not make co-determination a defining condition**; a remedy may work despite exclusion from making rules |
| **Michel Foucault** | Contrast between mobile power relations and states of domination where power is frozen and hardly reversible | Similar: not all power is domination; relational mechanisms matter | AP-0003 stresses normative arbitrariness more, normalization and closure of practices of freedom less |
| **Rainer Forst / right to justification** | Denial of equal standing in demanding and coauthoring justification for the rules governing people | Similar emphasis on justification and contestation | AP-0003 does not articulate standards of **equal justificatory standing** or rule-making participation |
| **Max Weber / `Herrschaft`** | Sociological authority and obedience to commands perceived as legitimate | Relevant to describing how authority and legitimacy work | A **fundamental semantic difference**: sociologically legitimate authority need not be morally wrongful; AP-0003 is a normatively critical concept |
| **Feminist and structural accounts** | Subordination through social norms, class, gender and other institutionalized differences; some examine subject formation and internalized constraints | Similar concern with systems, unintended effects and groups | Interference and appeal mechanisms may miss forms of culturally reproduced subordination |

**Source limits:** Pettit's views are summarized through the SEP; Lovett through publicly available publisher abstracts, not the full book; Young through the publisher information and academic summaries; Foucault through an English-language publication of the 1984 interview; Forst through McCammon rather than full primary-work review; and Weber through the SEP. Feminist accounts are not a single homogeneous position. Full references and limits follow below.

#### Key differences to keep under review

1. **Dependency: prerequisite or contextual factor?** AP-0003 can characterize credible arbitrary threats even when exit is relatively inexpensive. Dependency is constitutive for Lovett. Test examples rather than settling this intuitively.
2. **Control, justification and participation:** AP-0003 puts these safeguards together without stating which are individually necessary or sufficient. Procedural, democratic and equal-justification approaches may yield different outcomes.
3. **Systemic domination without a single identifiable master:** AP-0003 includes interacting mechanisms without a central controller. This is broader than some dyadic republican formulations but consistent with structural approaches. Identify relations and institutions rather than personifying a whole system.
4. **Domination versus oppression:** Young distinguishes overlapping but non-identical concepts. A harm or deprivation alone does not prove domination on AP-0003's criteria, and the contrast should be examined alongside AP-0002 on emancipation.
5. **Domination versus legitimacy:** Weber and AP-0003 use the same translated term for different analytical purposes. AP-0003 should state its normative use without treating Weberian authority as a direct refutation.
6. **Threshold and scope:** a slight ability to interfere with minor choices does not necessarily imply serious domination. Credibility, affected interests, dependency, exit options and long-term effects help test the classification.

**Research conclusion:** the sources do **not warrant automatically editing** the already submitted candidate text. At least three questions should remain visible at approval: the exact role of **dependency**, how **justification relates to control**, and whether **participation in forming the rules** belongs in the definition. A distinctive Anarchopoeia definition is an explicit, revisable decision, not a scholarly consensus.

### Contextual research framework — exploratory adaptation

This adaptation of the [Trialectic Method, `dev` reference of 16 November 2026](https://github.com/Nirgalz/trialectic/blob/3c3b5c831ce3afd86d7a5c654b167fe97502d30e/reference/trialectic-method.md) is a **proposed research aid**, not a requirement for subscribing to the manifesto or scientific validation of its definition. The reference examines Objects, NEEDs, CANs, Actualization conditions, Power (contextual evaluation), scenarios and effects. The political criteria of arbitrariness belong to AP-0003 and do not follow automatically from the model.

- **Subject and Environment:** choose an exposed individual or group as Main Subject and select relevant actors and systems as Secondary Subjects, without assuming the relationship is exclusively bilateral. Delimit context and time horizon.
- **NEEDs and CANs:** investigate the necessities and Potentialities of the relevant Objects. A necessity identified in one case is not automatically a universal NEED; a potential CAN is neither an observed action nor proof of arbitrary power.
- **Conditions and dependencies:** find the pathways and conditions that would make interference or challenge possible. Distinguish action conditions, assessed Power and Actualization; an absent condition does not establish universal impossibility.
- **Scenarios:** compare materially distinct responses (prevention, remedy, reducing dependency, changing rules), including favorable and adverse effects on relevant Subjects. A formal legal entitlement to challenge does not automatically make the relevant CAN actionable or a remedy effective.
- **Recursion:** after observed change, reassess relations, needs, potentialities and safeguards. Do not conflate hypothetical scenarios, observed changes and established causal attribution. Limit exploration while recording relevance, coverage and uncertainty.

### Hypothetical case study — landlord and tenant

Select the **tenant as Main Subject** and the landlord, appeal institutions and housing arrangements as Secondary Subjects. Examine a context-specific NEED to maintain housing in specified conditions; candidate CANs include the landlord's ability to initiate proceedings, the tenant's capacity to challenge them, and other parties' opportunities to provide remedies or alternative housing. Existence and action conditions require investigation in each legal and material context.

Do **not** infer domination from the landlord's CAN alone. Investigate the credibility of arbitrary interference, applicable safeguards, practical access to remedies, timing, costs and the tenant's material position. Compare at least three scenarios: improving remedies, reducing material dependency and modifying decision rules. Assess possible effects on both parties and others. This illustration **makes no factual legal or sociological finding** about a real case.

### Comparative questions for further research

- Compare freedom as non-domination, non-interference, structural theories of domination and accounts of arbitrary power.
- Investigate non-arbitrary asymmetries and justifiable collective constraints, including consent under dependency.
- Seek evidence distinguishing credible interference from an abstract possibility and effective remedies from purely formal rights.
- Assess unintended effects of safeguards, especially the concentration of new institutional powers.
- Examine contextual indications of the degree of domination without a universal normative score and document measurement difficulties.
- Record contradictory observations and sources, separating conclusions within a model from empirically substantiated claims.
