**Editorial note:** This proposal records one option drawn from an earlier article. It is open to revision, not a collectively endorsed conclusion.

## Problem

The article questions reward systems that may neglect hardship, danger, social usefulness or skill. Any scheme for 'fair' pay needs a defensible and contestable way to evaluate heterogeneous work.

## Objectives

Study remuneration systems that recognize diverse contributions and labor conditions without imposing a single external judgment of human worth.

## Proposal

Compare ways to incorporate working conditions, physical or psychological risk, essential social function, specialized knowledge and collective decision-making into pay structures. Separate the value of a person from the remuneration of an activity.

## Alternatives

Market wages, sectoral bargaining, negotiated pay scales, equal pay bands, worker-managed remuneration and mixed systems.

## Criticism and limitations

Social usefulness is contested and context-dependent. Rewarding dangerous work may normalize avoidable danger; technical expertise and hardship measures can create new hierarchies.

## Evaluation

Study inequality, living conditions, incentives for risk prevention, gender and social bias, transparency, appeals and effects on necessary occupations.

## Proposed Manifesto Entry

Anarchopoeia examines fair remuneration systems that take account of working conditions, risk, skills and social contribution. The criteria used to compensate an activity should be explicit, open to collective scrutiny and revisable, without being confused with the worth or dignity of the person performing it.

## Research

Review wage-setting theories and collective bargaining mechanisms; define risk and hardship indicators; examine conflicts between income guarantees and differentiated pay.
