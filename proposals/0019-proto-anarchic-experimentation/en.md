**Editorial note:** This proposal records one option drawn from an earlier article. It is open to revision, not a collectively endorsed conclusion.

## Problem

A prescriptive transition toward a fixed anarchist system may reproduce concentrated authority, suppress criticism or assume unrealistic timelines. The article advocates incremental experimentation instead.

## Objectives

Develop a continuous, evidence-informed process for changing institutions toward emancipation and non-domination, without guaranteeing a final stable state.

## Proposal

Explore pilot institutions, evaluation criteria, transparent revision cycles, rights of affected people, safeguards and termination conditions. The adjective 'proto-anarchic' denotes a working orientation, not a settled type of regime. Avoid assuming an anarchic arrangement necessarily requires generations or that transition can be completed in a few years.

## Alternatives

One-off constitutional redesign, abrupt revolution, incremental reforms without an explicit horizon, participatory experimentation and combinations thereof.

## Criticism and limitations

Experiments can impose risks on nonparticipants and produce temporary authorities that become entrenched. Scientific evaluation of contested social aims cannot rely on a single neutral metric.

## Evaluation

Assess participation, rights protections, meaningful exit and appeal, unintended effects, replicability, accountability and capacity to change or terminate the experiment.

## Proposed Manifesto Entry

Anarchopoeia pursues institutional transformation through a continuous process of inquiry, collective discussion, experimentation, evaluation and revision. Its anarchic horizon does not prescribe a final, permanently stable institutional order. Provisional arrangements should remain open to criticism, reversible where possible and accountable to those affected by them.

## Research

Develop ethical safeguards and decision criteria for political experiments; compare historical transitions, institutional learning and recursive governance; clarify diversity and cooperation ('unity through diversity') without treating the slogan as a settled principle.
