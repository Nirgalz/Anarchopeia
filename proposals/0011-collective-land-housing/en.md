**Editorial note:** This proposal records one option drawn from an earlier article. It is open to revision, not a collectively endorsed conclusion.

## Problem

Control over land and housing can shape access to essential resources and produce dependence. The initiating article calls for collectivization, without specifying rights and forms of governance.

## Objectives

Examine collective ownership, use rights and governance as possible ways to improve housing security, equitable land access and effective participation.

## Proposal

Compare arrangements for land and buildings under community, cooperative or publicly accountable stewardship. Distinguish legal ownership from occupancy, use rights, maintenance obligations and decision-making.

## Alternatives

Private ownership with regulation; housing cooperatives; community land trusts; municipal ownership; common-pool or mixed models.

## Criticism and limitations

Compulsory transfers raise difficult legal and distributive issues. Collective ownership may centralize allocation power or weaken tenants' autonomy if rights are not protected.

## Evaluation

Study affordability, tenure security, decision rights, mobility, maintenance, access for newcomers and protection against arbitrary eviction.

## Proposed Manifesto Entry

Anarchopoeia examines collective and cooperative arrangements for land and housing, with the aim of improving secure access and reducing dependence on arbitrary property power. Ownership and use arrangements must protect residents' rights, collective accountability and the ability to contest decisions.

## Research

Compare legal and practical models; investigate tenure, compensation, allocation and rights of minorities; distinguish rural land and urban housing cases.

### Historical notes to investigate — not adopted

The [founding principles](../../txt/principes%20fondateurs.txt) mention a **one-home-per-person** limit, collectively managed housing for mobility or low occupancy, public oversight and restricted inheritance. These are **strong additional policy hypotheses**, not agreed rules. Assess housing rights, compensation, residents' governance, family situations, mobility, inheritance law, vacancy and possible harms. Do not conflate residence, ownership and occupancy.
