**Draft:** Research proposal extracted from historical notes, not an approved position.

## Problem

A democratic investment choice requires knowledge of the labor, equipment and resources that a project consumes. The [historical economic model](../../txt/ecopolitique.txt) sketches several markets or accounting domains, yet does not establish how to prevent double-counting, scarcity, speculative manipulation or conflicting demand.

## Objectives

Explore transparent, plural accounting mechanisms that enable communities to compare and coordinate projects under limited resources, without concentrating planning authority or reducing all value to a single price.

## Proposal

Study a model distinguishing four related domains: **investment projects (I)**; **labor and skills (T)**; **physical resources and ecological constraints (R)**; and **productive equipment and capacity (P)**. Project proposals would describe expected inputs and ongoing needs, and scheduling would reflect scarce capacity, maintenance and ecological costs. These are **information and allocation domains**, not necessarily four tradeable currencies. The older text also imagines non-convertible currency units for separate markets; analyze this option against a common accounting unit and interoperable ledgers. Separate the question of **who allocates investment rights** (see [AP-0021](../0021-equal-investment-credits/en.md)) from **how feasible projects are evaluated**, and the question of **collecting supply/need data** (see [AP-0022](../0022-federated-needs-offers-protocol/en.md)) from any automated price or scheduling algorithm.

## Alternatives

Existing prices and contracts; participatory planning without separate units; cooperative supply chains; input-output accounting; physical-resource budgets; hybrid market and planning systems.

## Criticism and limitations

Different accounting units can become incomparable or be manipulated. Economically optimized allocations may violate rights or ecological thresholds; local preferences need not reveal long-term social needs. Expert-maintained algorithms can create hidden dependencies or central authority.

## Evaluation

Model project feasibility, resource contention, allocation fairness, ecological limits, data quality, transparency, dispute resolution, and whether citizens can understand and challenge costs and priorities.

## Proposed Manifesto Entry

Anarchopoeia studies transparent coordination of investment projects, work, resources and productive facilities. The information necessary to assess collective projects should be accessible and contestable, taking account of scarcity, ecological consequences, labor and the needs of affected communities without equating economic calculation with democratic legitimacy.

## Research

The [historical draft](../../txt/ecopolitique.txt) distinguishes investment, labor, resources and productive facilities and sketches localized resource pricing, committed investment subscriptions, nonconvertible denominations and aggregated demand. These are *design sketches*, not a tested economic architecture. Specify coherent units, double-entry or physical accounting, constraints, project lifecycles, scheduling algorithms and review rights. Evaluate compatibility with [externality assessment](../0017-social-environmental-externalities/en.md) and [collective productive assets](../0012-collective-production-assets/en.md).
