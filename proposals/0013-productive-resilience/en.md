**Editorial note:** This proposal records one option drawn from an earlier article. It is open to revision, not a collectively endorsed conclusion.

## Problem

The article associates external economic pressure with dependence on essential supplies and production. That concern requires differentiation by sector and scale.

## Objectives

Explore ways for communities to retain reliable access to essential goods and services and resist coercive dependencies without defaulting to autarky.

## Proposal

Compare approaches to food, energy and industrial security: diversified supply, local and regional capacity, cooperative ownership, strategic reserves, public infrastructure and reciprocal interregional agreements. Distinguish autonomy, sovereignty, resilience and self-sufficiency.

## Alternatives

Globalized specialization with safeguards; strategic autonomy; regional cooperation; localized production; mixed strategies.

## Criticism and limitations

Overly local production may raise costs, environmental impacts or vulnerability to local shocks. The meaning of 'industrial sovereignty' varies across needs and jurisdictions.

## Evaluation

Assess continuity of supply, ecological effects, affordability, dependence on concentrated suppliers, workforce conditions and ability to respond to disruptions.

## Proposed Manifesto Entry

Anarchopoeia explores food, energy and industrial resilience to reduce dependencies that expose communities to arbitrary external pressure. Reliable access to essentials can combine local capabilities, diversified supply chains and cooperation across territories, subject to ecological and social evaluation.

## Research

Separate the three sectors for detailed studies; map critical dependencies, supply risks and environmental tradeoffs; study collective planning versus redundancy and specialization.
