**Editorial note:** This proposal records one option drawn from an earlier article. It is open to revision, not a collectively endorsed conclusion.

## Problem

The original text argues that monetary creation can centralize economic power and proposes sharing it more equally. The existing monetary arrangements and the distributional consequences must be documented.

## Objectives

Investigate monetary systems in which creation rules are transparent, distributed or governed through accountable arrangements.

## Proposal

Compare designs for issuing new currencies or monetary units under collectively determined rules. Separate the allocation of new money from a guaranteed income, which may have other funding sources. Specify issuance, governance, redemption and exchange mechanisms before implementation.

## Alternatives

Central-bank issuance, bank credit, mutual credit, local currencies, monetary dividends and public financing mechanisms.

## Criticism and limitations

New currencies may face adoption, legal and liquidity challenges; equal issuance does not ensure equal purchasing power. Inflation, monetary sovereignty and systemic risk need analysis.

## Evaluation

Model issuance and purchasing-power effects, governance capture risks, convertibility, payment access, price stability and feasibility in actual jurisdictions.

## Proposed Manifesto Entry

Anarchopoeia investigates systems of monetary creation whose rules and distribution can be governed transparently and collectively. Monetary design must be evaluated by its effects on economic power, access to resources, stability and the capacity of participants to contest the rules.

## Research

Document how contemporary money is created; compare proposals for shared issuance; investigate macroeconomic constraints, monetary law and governance of currency systems.

### Historical notes to investigate — not adopted

The [economic-political notes](https://github.com/Nirgalz/Anarchopeia/blob/0d8963af515be54957f767531017aff3d6881955/txt/ecopolitique.txt) illustrate **periodic equal per-person money creation** for consumption; “100 units per month” is merely an example. Study issuance eligibility, inflation, unequal needs, existing currencies and the difference between equal issuance and equitable outcomes. The notes introduce a separate **investment allocation unit**; examine it in its own proposal, not as a synonym for consumption money.
