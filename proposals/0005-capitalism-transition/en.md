**Editorial note:** This proposal records one option drawn from an earlier article. It is open to revision, not a collectively endorsed conclusion.

## Problem

The initiating article argues that redistributive reforms alone may leave economic dependence and concentrations of power unchanged. That diagnosis needs to be tested rather than assumed.

## Objectives

Assess whether and how changes beyond capitalist relations could advance emancipation and non-domination without assuming that all non-capitalist systems do so.

## Proposal

Investigate a transition away from capitalist ownership, financing and production arrangements. Distinguish the normative aim, diagnoses of existing mechanisms, candidate institutional alternatives and possible transition paths. Do not presume a universal definition of capitalism or an automatic consensus on its abolition.

## Alternatives

Reformed market capitalism; mixed economies; cooperative or commons-based models; other arrangements with diverse ownership structures.

## Criticism and limitations

The scope of 'capitalism' is contested. Replacing private owners with centralized institutions could reproduce domination. Transition costs, coordination and unintended consequences require investigation.

## Evaluation

Compare control over productive resources, effective freedom, livelihood security, accountability, ecological effects and the reversibility of institutional changes.

## Proposed Manifesto Entry

Anarchopoeia examines the possibility of moving beyond capitalism by transforming the institutions that govern ownership, financing and production. Alternatives are assessed by their contribution to individual and collective emancipation and to the reduction of domination, not by their ideological label. Such changes must remain contestable, evaluable and revisable.

## Research

Define the competing meanings of capitalism; identify testable claims about exploitation and inequality; compare historical transformations and institutional alternatives; analyze interactions with property, money and governance.

### Historical notes to investigate — not adopted

The [introduction](https://github.com/Nirgalz/Anarchopeia/blob/0d8963af515be54957f767531017aff3d6881955/txt/intro.txt), [critiques](https://github.com/Nirgalz/Anarchopeia/blob/0d8963af515be54957f767531017aff3d6881955/txt/critiques.txt) and [moral notes](https://github.com/Nirgalz/Anarchopeia/blob/0d8963af515be54957f767531017aff3d6881955/txt/morale.txt) question profit imperatives, social narratives of individual success and compulsory market participation. Their claims about the intentions of social classes, universal exploitation and population majorities are **unverified**. Distinguish structural incentives from moralized collective stereotypes, and investigate whether alternatives create new hierarchies.
