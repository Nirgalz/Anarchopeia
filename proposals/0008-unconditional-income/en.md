**Editorial note:** This proposal records one option drawn from an earlier article. It is open to revision, not a collectively endorsed conclusion.

## Problem

Dependence on employment or conditional benefits can restrict effective choices and livelihood security. The article associates an unconditional income with monetary creation, a link that should not be made obligatory.

## Objectives

Explore mechanisms for a predictable individual income independent of employment status or participation in prescribed activities.

## Proposal

Compare unconditional cash transfers and other guaranteed-income models, including eligibility, residence, financing, interactions with public services, taxation and nonmonetary needs. Preserve the distinction between a guaranteed payment and genuine access to resources.

## Alternatives

Targeted benefits; universal basic services; negative income tax; social dividends; job guarantees; combinations of these.

## Criticism and limitations

Costs, price responses, interactions with services and distributional effects vary by design. A cash income alone may not overcome housing, care or accessibility barriers.

## Evaluation

Examine poverty, material security, bargaining power, administrative burden, health, access to services and long-term funding under alternative designs.

## Proposed Manifesto Entry

Anarchopoeia explores an unconditional individual income as a possible means of strengthening material security and the freedom to choose one's activities. Its design and financing should be compared with other forms of social provision and evaluated for their effects on emancipation, dependency and access to essential resources.

## Research

Review comparative models and empirical studies of basic income; clarify universality versus unconditionality; explore alternative fiscal and monetary financing.
